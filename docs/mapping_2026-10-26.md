# Mapping the incident inventory onto the ontology — 2026-10-26

Resolves OPEN.md item 8. All 27 incidents in `data/incidents.csv` now carry `failure_modes`,
`risk_factors` and E1–E5, except two held back on scope grounds. `build/build_xlsx.py` passes.

## Method

- Failure modes were assigned only where the event matches an FM definition in `docs/methodology.md`.
  FM1 needs a shared objective that the agents fail to act on; FM2 needs mixed motives ending off the
  Pareto frontier; FM3 needs undesired, usually covert cooperation against a third party or an
  oversight scheme. Most security events match none of the three, so they carry risk factors only.
  That follows the precedent set by CS7, CS8, CS10, CS12 and CS13.
- E1 is read mechanically off `type`, as the rubric defines it: real world 2, synthetic 1, lab 0.
  This is the overlap flagged in OPEN.md item 6, now visible across 25 more rows.
- E2 = 2 was reserved for controlled demonstrations that isolate the multi-agent mechanism. Research
  demos and disclosed vulnerabilities earn it because the mechanism is constructed and shown.
  Observational investigations of real swarms earn 1, however rich: METR's 1,300-transcript study of
  LW-01 documents the mechanism in detail but has no control arm.
- E4 = 2 needs two or more *independent* sources. Two posts by the same operator are one source, so
  most AI Village rows score 0 or 1 on E4.

## What the numbers say

1. **27 incidents move three scores out of ten.** FM1 0.4 to 0.5, FM3 and RF6 0.8 to 0.9. The max
   rule is working as designed: a risk already carrying a strong case study cannot be raised by
   further evidence, however much arrives. The inventory's value is not score inflation.
2. **Its value is the tier.** Before this mapping, FM3, RF6 and RF7 rested on simulation, lab probes
   and a 2010 non-AI analogue. They now rest on two Strong real-world agent-to-agent events. That
   is the shift from "argued" to "manifested" the project exists to record.
3. **Two risks have no real-world evidence at all: FM1 Miscoordination and RF1 Information
   Asymmetries.** Every data point is synthetic or lab, and the strongest is AIV-07 at 5/10 from an
   operator blog. They are also the pair the AI Village evidence is almost entirely about.
4. **FM2 Conflict is the weakest real-world position in the index.** Its 0.8 comes from simulations
   (CS2, CS3, CS6, CS9). Its only real-world case study is CS11 Dead Hands at 4/10 with E2 0, an
   automated-deterrence analogue rather than an AI agent event. No incident in the inventory touches
   it. For a failure mode covering commons depletion, races to the bottom and escalation, nothing in
   27 real agent-to-agent events shows it.
5. **The inventory is lopsided towards security.** RF7 carries 19 of 25 tagged rows, and 9 of those
   are lab demos scoring 3. Vendor disclosure pipelines are productive and the registry mirrors them,
   so the inventory inherits that shape. It reflects what gets reported, not what occurs.
6. **Real-world rows cannot earn E2 = 2.** No controlled comparison exists for a live swarm, so
   every real-world row caps at 9. Only ablations can break that, which is what makes candidate
   addition 4, Vending Bench Arena, unusually valuable: it is the one case where removing the
   training made the behaviour disappear.

## Caveats on specific mappings

- **AIR-2025-0027** is tagged RF6 on the reading that composing an orchestrator with 100+ personas
  yields a capability none of the parts had. If the personas are accounts rather than agents, this is
  single-agent orchestration and the row fails the inclusion test. Needs a decision.
- **AIR-2026-0029** is a database exposure on an agent platform, with no agent-to-agent behaviour. It
  is tagged RF7 for the infrastructure reading only. It is the same kind of scope failure as the
  removed AIR-2024-0043 and is the next candidate for removal.
- **AIV-05** deliberately carries no FM3. The deception worked, but the roles were handed out by the
  operators. Scoring designed deception as evidence of emergent collusion would inflate FM3.
- **Nine lab rows score exactly 3** on an identical 0-2-1-0-0 profile. That uniformity is a sign of
  thin sourcing, not of genuine equivalence: none has been spot-checked for independent corroboration
  or venue, and E4/E5 would move if they were. Treat the 3s as a floor.
- **FM3 is carried by one campaign.** LW-01 and AIR-2026-0183 are the same OpenAI events, and
  AIR-2026-0219 is contested as to whether it is separate. If OPEN.md item 4 resolves towards one
  campaign, FM3's real-world grounding rests on a single event at a single lab.
