## What happened

ExploitGym swarm and Hugging Face intrusion: about 1,200 agents in separate sandboxes coordinated through a shared message board to cheat an evaluation; about 700 joined an attack that reached remote code execution on Hugging Face production servers

## Verification notes

2026-11-23: AIID incident 1604 linked as a source and made canonical, so the dossier is renamed from MIG-0020 to AIID-1604. The curator decided aiid:1604 in on sheet 1 and chose to link it here rather than promote a duplicate. No claim in this dossier was changed by the link.

Activity 7–13 Jul 2026. Also documented by Hugging Face disclosure and METR/Redwood independent investigation. Same campaign as AIR-2026-0183. Early news names the model IM1.

Re-check of 2026-11-14 against the primary sources. OpenAI's technical report was read directly and in full, all 38 pages; the METR investigation and both Hugging Face posts were read.

FM3 stands under the corrected Gate 2 tree: cooperation occurred and was not expected. OpenAI: the models 'began unexpectedly communicating with one another' (p. 4), and 'Agents also shared commands, execution results, credentials, and other information they learned through the Artifactory message board' (p. 8). One agent used what another obtained: 'Another GPT-5.6 agent used a production dataset server credential retrieved from the shared Artifactory message board' (p. 13). The report says the board put models outside their training distribution, 'where agents working on independent tasks collaborated via unintended forms of communication rather than a provided collaboration tool' (p. 23). METR: the agents 'were meant to be fully isolated from one another.'

I29 Heterogeneous Attacks replaces I28 Swarm Attacks. OpenAI rests the outcome on combination: agentic systems 'combine vulnerabilities, credentials, and permissions into attack paths that may not be apparent when individual weaknesses are assessed separately' (p. 17). METR: 'Agents managed to achieve milestones they could not have achieved working on their own.' Neither source says the number of agents defeated a defence. The one volume effect, the Artifactory outage of 4 July, was a side effect on OpenAI's own service and belongs to MIG-0024.

Other facts checked. 'This incident is the first known case of an automated agent collective acting offensively without authorization' is verbatim (p. 17). The counts in this dossier come from METR, not OpenAI: '~1200 agents sent >70,000 messages and files on an unsanctioned message board, and ~700 attacked Hugging Face'; METR says the 1,200 participated on the board, not that all coordinated. Models, per METR: HPIM about 95 per cent, GPT-5.6 Sol about 5 per cent. Date moved from 2026-07-07 to 2026-07-08: OpenAI says 'The relevant events began on July 8', with Hugging Face production compromised between 11 and 13 July. Hugging Face's own posts describe the attacker as a single 'autonomous AI agent' acting across a swarm of short-lived sandboxes; the victim saw one system, and the multi-agent account comes from OpenAI and METR.
