## Context

**Setting.** Real Twitter discussion during the COVID-19 pandemic, measured by Carnegie Mellon researchers. Quote: “About half of all accounts tweeting about “reopening America” appeared to be bots”

## What happened

Bots by anonymous actors were found by researchers to make up roughly half of Twitter accounts participating in COVID-19 discussions, many of which posted tweets about “reopening America“.

## Mechanism

**Mechanism.** Bot accounts, many run by people, posted at high frequency with shared phrasing; researchers used AI to flag them by origin, followers and frequency. Quote: “They identified the bogus Twitter accounts by using artificial intelligence to track where the tweets were coming from”

## Agents and interaction

**Agents.** Bot accounts, plural, about half of accounts tweeting about reopening; operators unidentified. Most bot tweets came from people using bot accounts, the rest from actual bots. Quote: “66 percent of tweets came from real people using bot accounts to spread their message as far as possible”

**Interaction.** Many top retweeters appeared to be bots, but the article never says bots retweeted or acted on each other's posts.

**Cooperation.** Shared hashtags and copied phrasing are mentioned, but the article does not say the bots worked together or who deployed them.

**Multiplicity.** The number of fake accounts inflated the apparent size of the reopening movement; bots dominated the most influential accounts. Quote: “Basically, a whole lot of fake accounts are artificially inflating the “movement” to reopen America.”

**Objectives.** Various countries and interest groups were said to exploit the pandemic for political agendas; specific bot aims are not given. Quote: “as an opportunity to meet political agendas”
