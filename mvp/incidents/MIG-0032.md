## Context

**Setting.** Real Twitter platform during the Russia-Ukraine war. Quote: “liking tweets pushing pro-Russian justifications for the war”

## What happened

The Russian government has been stepping up its foreign influence campaigns by using artificial intelligence and emerging technologies to spread disinformation and sow distrust in policies supportive of Ukraine. Part of the strategy includes carrying out influence laundering operations by disseminating their messages to the American public via allies inside nominally independent organizations, according to a recent declassified analysis.

## Mechanism

**Mechanism.** Bot accounts, many created around the invasion, liked Russian government tweets to boost them and game platform trends; researchers scored them with Botometer. Quote: “There were hundreds — 870 accounts had a score of one.”

## Agents and interaction

**Agents.** Thousands of automated Twitter accounts (bots) of unidentified operators; the article does not say they used AI, only that a machine-learning tool (Botometer) was used to detect them. Quote: “An army of automated Twitter accounts, or bots, stood ready to promote these posts and game the platform”

**Interaction.** The bots liked tweets from Russian government and embassy accounts; the article does not describe bots acting on other bots' posts.

**Cooperation.** The article does not say whether the bots were coordinated by one operator or acted together beyond liking the same tweets.

**Multiplicity.** The number of bot accounts is credited with pushing a hashtag to trend. Quote: “The fact the hashtag was trending in early March shows the power of bot accounts to manipulate platforms, he said.”

**Objectives.** Shared aim: boost pro-Russian posts. Quote: “liking tweets pushing pro-Russian justifications for the war”
